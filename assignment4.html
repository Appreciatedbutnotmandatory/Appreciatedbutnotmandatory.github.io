Deltarune (2018) is an independent role playing video game divided into multiple chapters, developed by Toby Fox, which centers around the narrative and its characters. The story revolves around a small group of teenagers, who discover in their school a getaway to a parallel world, called the Dark World. In the Dark World, the teenagers gain the magical power to fight, their goal being to escape and go back to their original world, the Light World. The Darkners, the Dark World residents, are all personifications of items found in the Light World. For example, the first chapter takes place in an unused storage room, filled with board games and card games – thus all the Darkners are based on chess pieces, checkers pieces, jigsaw puzzles, playing cards, etc…

The main appeal of the game is a simple moral choice given to the player: they can fight and kill the enemies, or act with kindness and spare them. This choice is omnipresent, it defines the entire game experience and narration. Every non-playable character (or NPCs) are appealing and endearing, with unique characteristics to make them stand out, often through speech mannerisms.

A great example would be a fan-favorite character called Spamton G. Spamton, or simply Spamton for short. In the second chapter of the game, the protagonists explore a second Dark World nicknamed the Cyber World, this time located in the library’s computer lab. Every Darkner in the Cyber World is themed around computers and the internet, like Virovirokuns (computer viruses) and their mortal enemies Ambyu-Lances (antivirus programs), Tasque Manager (task manager), or Addisons (advertisements), etc …

Exploring through the Cyber World, the player meets Spamton in an alley-way filled with dumpsters. He presents himself as a business man, who once was a big shot, desperate to make a deal, any deal, with the player. Like his name suggests, he is a personification of spam e-mails, trying ceaselessly to scam someone out of their personal information or money. He once was very successful, but gradually his sales went down the drain, and now he has nothing to his name, his home reduced to a dumpster in an alley-way (precisely like spam e-mails once successful, but now automatically detected and sent to the trash).

Spamton stands out from other characters thanks to his peculiar speech impairment. Indeed, his dialogue texts are filled with typos and formatting errors, simulating a stutter and a struggle articulating. His introduction speech starts as follows:

* HEY      EVERY      !! IT'S ME!!!

* EV3RY   BUDDY   'S FAVORITE [[Number 1 Rated Salesman1997]]

* SPAMT

* SPAMTON G. SPAMTON!!

* WOAH!! IF IT ISN"T A...

* LIGHT nER! HEY-HE Y HEY!!!

From the very first line, multiple quirks can be pointed out. Spamton comes across as very intense – talking exclusively in uppercase letters, every sentence finishing with multiple exclamation points – demanding the player’s entire attention on him. His text has irregular spacing, here there are large non-conventional gaps between “HEY”, “EVERY” and “!!”, which go back to normal a few words later. This opening sentence appears incomplete, Spamton uses “EVERY” as a determiner to no nouns, the spacing suggests a noun should have been there, but he wasn’t able to utter any.

Afterwards, he uses “EV3RY BUDDY ‘S”, a deformation of “everybody”. The “3” in “EV3RY” imitates both a typo – the “3” key is next to the “e” key on QWERTY keyboards, making it easy to slip and press the wrong one – and leetspeak, an alternative way of spelling words with numbers instead of letters, used mainly by hackers on the Internet in the 80s. The reference to leetspeak, a former, no longer popular nor useful way of communicating, hints at Spamton’s obsolescence.

When trying to say his own name, Spamton stutters, having to start another dialogue line to properly speak.

Spamton tries to pronounce “lightner” – referring to a person who comes from the Light World, like our main playable character – however he fails at articulating properly again, warping the word into “LIGHT nER”, suggesting a struggle to pronounce the second syllable.

The three “HEY”s in a row – formatted incorrectly, like Spamton’s voice was tripping on itself in a stutter – further suggests how adamant he is about demanding the player’s attention.

The last major quirk to consider is the text in double brackets. Indeed, this oddity comes back throughout all of Spamton’s dialogues. Here are a few examples:

* EV3RY   BUDDY  'S FAVORITE [[Number 1 Rated Salesman1997]]

* LOOKS LIKE YOU'RE [[All Alone On A Late Night?]]

* YOU WANT IT. YOU WANT [[Hyperlink Blocked]], DON'T YOU.

The text in double brackets, resembling ad-like interruptions, stands out for multiple reasons. First, it is the only circumstance in which Spamton does not speak in full uppercase. The beginning of each word is capitalised, still overtly desperate to grab the attention of anyone listening, however it comes off as less intense and more intelligible.

The other main attribute making those texts stand out, is of course the double brackets. Enclosing text in double brackets is a convention in the Markdown language – a markup language used to format plain text, like on blogs, instant messaging, forums, etc… – which adds a hyperlink in a text. This closely relates to Spamton being a personification of spam e-mails, he tries to trick someone into clicking a link to a phishing website. This quirk takes advantage of the written text format, as expressing it in a vocalised dialogue would be impossible. The third example confirms that the double brackets stem from the Markdown convention, directly indicating its nature as a hyperlink. However, the hyperlink Spamton wished to say is blocked, obscuring the object of his sentence, thus making it impossible to fully understand his offer.

It is left ambiguous whether Spamton is aware of his ad-like interruption while he speaks; he never corrects himself when his sentences make no sense, suggesting that he is not conscious of the disparity between what he wanted to say and what he actually expressed.

As demonstrated with Spamton’s introduction dialogue, Toby Fox – Deltarune’s creator – is not afraid to break standard English convention if it helps the characterisation and memorability of its numerous NPCs. To exceed conventional English also allows speech-related disabilities to be represented, an important way to support and bring awareness to a marginalised group.
